Empowering Women and Girls with Disabilities to Lead Change in Nsanje

Nsanje District, Malawi | August 2025 – July 2026

📣

Project overview

CAPDI is strengthening the advocacy capacity of women and girls with disabilities in Nsanje.

Women and girls with disabilities in rural communities often face multiple barriers to participating in decisions that affect their lives. Limited access to information, social and family barriers, inaccessible services and few opportunities to engage decision-makers can leave their voices unheard. In response to these challenges, Caring for Persons with Disabilities (CAPDI) implemented the project “Empowering Women and Girls with Disabilities: Advocacy for Gender Equality and Inclusion” in Nsanje District, with support from the Women and Girls Fund (WGF).

The project focused on strengthening the advocacy capacity of women and girls with disabilities while creating opportunities for them to engage community leaders, government authorities and service providers on issues affecting their rights, participation and access to services, including education, health and justice.

🧠

Building confidence, knowledge and advocacy skills

Thirty women and girls with disabilities received training in advocacy, rights and policy engagement.

The training provided participants with practical knowledge and accessible learning approaches. Participants demonstrated greater confidence and willingness to speak about issues affecting them and engage with community structures.

🗣️

Bringing communities and leaders into the conversation

Community leaders and male counterparts were engaged to support disability inclusion and gender equality.

CAPDI engaged 34 traditional and community leaders, exceeding the original target of 28, and 40 male counterparts to promote greater understanding of disability inclusion, gender equality and participation.

♿

Strengthening engagement with government

Dialogue platforms and accessibility audits helped communities engage government and service providers.

CAPDI engaged 20 government officials and service providers, established 5 dialogue platforms, and conducted 10 accessibility audits of public facilities.

📢

Taking disability inclusion to communities

Public awareness activities reached approximately 2,500 people in targeted communities.

The activities raised awareness about the rights and needs of women and girls with disabilities. CAPDI also developed and distributed 30 inclusive policy implementation toolkits and guidelines.

✅

Strengthening accountability and participation

Monitoring activities created practical ways for communities to engage duty-bearers and track progress.

CAPDI engaged 40 members of the District Executive Committee, developed a service-provider scorecard, and conducted three project site visits to encourage accountability among service providers.

🛠️

Strengthening CAPDI’s capacity

CAPDI improved its organisational capacity to continue disability-inclusive advocacy beyond the project.

Ten CAPDI staff received training in monitoring and evaluation and resource mobilisation. The organisation also improved its website, developed advocacy materials and procured essential office equipment.

🚲

Turning challenges into opportunities

CAPDI worked with families and local leaders to overcome participation and transport barriers.

When some families resisted women’s participation, CAPDI engaged the Area Development Committee Chairperson to explain the training and emphasise participants’ capabilities and rights. Transport support, including local bicycle operators, helped participants travel safely from remote communities.

👥

What participants gained

Participants gained rights knowledge, confidence, advocacy skills and more opportunities to engage decision-makers.

Through training, dialogue and community engagement, participants gained:

  • Clear understanding of their rights and legal protections.
  • Increased confidence to speak about issues affecting them.
  • Greater willingness to engage community and government structures.
  • Improved advocacy and mobilisation skills.
  • More opportunities to participate in dialogue with decision-makers.
🌱

A foundation for sustained inclusion

The project amplified the voices of women and girls with disabilities and created a foundation for sustained inclusion.

The Nsanje experience demonstrates that when women and girls with disabilities receive appropriate information, accessibility and supportive platforms, they are willing and able to participate in advocacy. CAPDI has strengthened its capacity for future work on gender equality, disability inclusion and the rights of women and girls with disabilities.
